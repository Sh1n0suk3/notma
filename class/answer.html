Question 1

What property of this wave is represented by the letter "A"?

wavelength

The letter "A" measures the horizontal distance from one point on the wave (like a trough) to the identical point on the next wave. This specific distance is defined as the wavelength, often represented by the Greek letter lambda (\(\lambda\)).

Question 2

What property of the wave is represented by the letter "B"?

amplitude

The letter "B" indicates the maximum displacement, or vertical distance, from the wave's equilibrium position (the midpoint) to its crest. This is the definition of amplitude, which relates to the wave's energy.

Question 3

What kind of wave is pictured below?

transverse wave

Assuming the image shows a standard wave shape, the particles of the medium would be moving up and down while the wave's energy moves horizontally. When particle motion is perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation, it is a transverse wave.

Question 4

Which letter(s) represent the trough of the wave?

D

The trough is the lowest point on a wave. In the given diagram, the letter "D" is clearly pointing to the bottom-most part of the wave cycle.

Question 5

Which letter(s) represent the crest of the wave?

B & F

The crest is the highest point on a wave. The letters "B" and "F" both indicate the peaks, or highest points, of their respective wave cycles in the diagram.

Question 6

Which wave in the diagram has the greatest frequency?

4

Frequency is the number of waves that pass a point in a given amount of time. Wave #4 has the most waves compressed into the same space, meaning it has the highest frequency.

Question 7

The top image shows compressions and rarefactions, where particle motion is parallel to the wave's direction; this is a longitudinal wave (like sound). The bottom image shows particle motion perpendicular to the wave's direction; this is a transverse wave (like light).

Question 9

Question 10

As frequency increases, the wavelength ___?

decreases

Frequency and wavelength have an inverse relationship for a wave traveling at a constant speed (\(v = f\lambda\)). This means that if you fit more waves into a given time (higher frequency), the length of each individual wave must get shorter (smaller wavelength).

Health

Questions 10

Question 1

Repeated cell divisions during embryonic cell development is known as?

Cleavage

Cleavage is the specific term for the rapid, successive mitotic cell divisions of a zygote that occur without an increase in the overall size of the embryo.

Question 2

The joining of a sperm cell and an egg cell is called?

Fertilization

Fertilization is the biological process of fusing a male gamete (sperm) with a female gamete (egg) to initiate the development of a new organism.

Question 3

Which of the following sequences represents the correct order of events in the formation of a human fetus?

Fertilization → Zygote → Blastocyst → Embryo

Development begins with Fertilization, creating a single-celled Zygote. This cell divides to form a Blastocyst, which then implants and develops into the Embryo.

Question 4

What is the name of this process?

meiosis

The diagram shows a single parent cell dividing twice to produce four unique daughter cells with half the original number of chromosomes. This process is meiosis, which creates gametes (sperm and egg cells).

Question 5

In which of the following structures of the female human reproductive system is the blastocyst implanted during normal human development?

uterus

After traveling down the fallopian tube, the blastocyst attaches to and embeds itself into the nutrient-rich lining of the uterus, a process called implantation.

Question 6

The diagram below represents some stages of early embryonic development. Which process is represented by the arrows in the diagram?

Mitosis

Ma note:The diagram is not provided.

Question 7

A fertilized egg undergoes several stages before it is implanted. In which of the following structure of the female reproductive system is the egg fertilized?

Fallopian Tubes

Fertilization typically occurs in the fallopian tubes (oviducts), where the egg travels after being released from the ovary and meets the sperm.

Question 8

What happens to the zygote immediately after fertilization?

It divides by mitosis to form a blastocyst.

The single-celled zygote immediately begins a series of rapid cell divisions (cleavage) via mitosis. This process eventually forms a hollow ball of cells called a blastocyst.

Question 9

What is the germinal stage of pregnancy?

The first two weeks after conception

The germinal stage is the earliest phase of prenatal development, covering the period from fertilization until the blastocyst completes implantation in the uterus, which is approximately the first two weeks.

Question 10

What is the term for the fertilized egg?

Zygote

A Zygote is the specific scientific term for the single diploid cell that results from the fusion of an egg and a sperm during fertilization.

Answers for HW#2 Embryo

Health

Questions 10

Question 1

Which layer gives rise to the nervous system?

Ectoderm

The ectoderm is the outermost of the three primary germ layers. It is responsible for developing into the skin, nails, hair, and the entire nervous system, including the brain and spinal cord.

Question 2

What is this process called during week 3?

Neurulation

The image depicts the formation of the neural tube from the ectoderm. This crucial process, which establishes the central nervous system, is called neurulation and is a key event of the third week.

Question 3

The heart begins to beat around?

Week 3

One of the earliest major milestones, the primitive heart tube forms and begins to beat and pump blood during the third week of gestation, often before the pregnancy is even confirmed.

Question 4

Limb buds first become visible during?

Week 6

By week 6, the small paddle-like limb buds that will develop into arms and legs become visible. This marks the beginning of limb formation.

Question 5

Around what size is the human embryo by week 3?

Poppy seed

At the end of the third week, the embryo is incredibly small, measuring about 1.5 to 2.5 millimeters, which is comparable to the size of a poppy seed.

Question 6

The embryo reaches the size of a blueberry at approximately?

Week 7

By week 7, the embryo has grown significantly and measures about 1/2 inch (1.25 cm), roughly the size of a blueberry.

Question 7

Which of the following is the main event of neurulation?

Formation of the spinal cord

Neurulation is the process where the neural plate folds inward to form the neural tube. This tube is the precursor to the entire central nervous system, which includes the brain and, crucially, the spinal cord.

Question 8

The presence of a tail in human embryos provides evidence that humans share ancestry with?

Fish and other vertebrates

Human embryos temporarily have a tail, which later recedes. This feature, known as an evolutionary vestige, reflects a shared genetic history with fish and other vertebrates that retain a tail into adulthood.

Question 9

What embryonic feature do fish and humans share early in development?

gill slits

Both fish and human embryos possess pharyngeal arches, often called gill slits. In fish, they develop into gills, while in humans, they develop into structures of the head, neck, and ear, indicating a common evolutionary origin.

Question 10

Why do early embryos of vertebrates look alike?

They inherited genes from a common ancestor

The striking similarities between early vertebrate embryos (like humans, fish, and chickens) are due to them sharing ancient developmental genes. They inherited these genes from a common ancestor, and these genes guide the foundational stages of body plan formation.

Questions 10

Question 1

Biotic or abiotic factor?

abiotic

The image shows soil, which is composed of non-living materials like minerals, water, and air. Therefore, soil is a foundational abiotic factor in most terrestrial ecosystems.

Question 2

Biotic or abiotic factor?

abiotic

The image depicts wind. Wind is the movement of air in the atmosphere; it is a non-living climatic condition, making it a key abiotic factor that affects organisms.

Question 3

What is the organism shown in the picture?

producer

The image shows a green leaf, which belongs to a plant. Plants create their own food through photosynthesis. In an ecosystem, organisms that produce their own food are called producers.

Question 4

What is the organism shown in the picture?

decomposer

The image shows mushrooms, which are a type of fungus. Fungi get their nutrients by breaking down dead organic matter, recycling it back into the ecosystem. This role makes them essential decomposers.

Question 5

What process is shown in the picture?

decomposition

The image shows a dead animal being broken down and returning to the earth. This natural process of decay is called decomposition.

Question 6

What is the organism shown in the picture?

autotroph

The image shows a flower, which is part of a plant. An autotroph is any organism that can produce its own food, typically using light energy. Plants are the most common examples of autotrophs.

Question 7

What are two biotic factors interacting with each other?

a rabbit eating grass

Biotic factors are living or once-living. Both a rabbit (living animal) and grass (living plant) are biotic. Eating is a direct interaction between these two biotic factors. The other options involve an abiotic factor (soil, water, sand).

Question 8

Which one represents an biotic factor interacting with an abiotic factor?

a tree uses sunlight for photosynthesis

A tree is a living organism (biotic factor). Sunlight is a non-living energy source (abiotic factor). Photosynthesis is the process where the tree uses sunlight to create food, representing a direct interaction between a biotic and an abiotic factor.

Question 9

Biotic or abiotic factor?

biotic

The image shows a log. Because the log was once part of a living tree, it is considered a biotic factor. Biotic factors include all living and once-living components of an ecosystem.

Question 10

Abiotic factors can affect biotic factors and biotic factors can affect abiotic factors.

true

This statement is true. For example, the amount of rainfall (abiotic) affects plant growth (biotic), and the decomposition of dead plants (biotic) can change the nutrient composition of the soil (abiotic). Ecosystems are defined by these complex interactions.
